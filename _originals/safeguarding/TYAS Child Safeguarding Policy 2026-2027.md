TYAS Child Safeguarding Policy

# 

Safeguarding is a set of organizational policies, procedures, and practices designed to ensure a safe environment for people at TYAS, including but not limited to:

* Protecting children from maltreatment  
* Prevent impaired development  
* Safe and effective care  
* Take action to reduce issues

It is the ethical and moral responsibility of the school to create a safe environment for all TYAS students. TYAS expects that adults in its employ will recognize that students’ safety, welfare, and health are our highest priority. It is the responsibility of adults charged with any aspect of the care and teaching of students to ensure that interactions among all members of the community are appropriate. Adults associated with the School must ensure that they model attitudes and behaviors that reflect their awareness of this obligation, and create an environment in which the best interests of students are the highest priority.

It is the policy of TYAS to provide and maintain a workplace that is free of any form of harassment, a responsibility shared by both employees and students at the school. All employees of TYAS are expected to demonstrate appropriate social media use and communication with students. Specifically, teachers should not accept social media invitations from students nor follow any students on social media until they graduate from the school. This policy applies to all students, employees of TYAS, parents, contract workers, and external providers.

It is a violation of TYAS policy to retaliate against an Employee for making or filing a complaint of harassment, or for cooperating in the investigation related to the safeguarding of a child. Any such retaliation will result in disciplinary action, up to and including immediate termination of employment.

At TYAS there are two key aspects to promoting the welfare of children:

1. Take all reasonable measures to ensure that the risk of harm to children’s welfare is minimized.  
2. Take all appropriate actions to address concerns about the welfare of children in accordance with local policies and procedures set out by the appropriate Taiwanese government agencies.

# **Taiwan and Child Protection** {#taiwan-and-child-protection}

TYAS is located in Taiwan, where the school complies with and supports child protection as the R.O.C. government requires.

**TYAS has shaped its definition of a safe child based on the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989):** 

Every child has the right to be safe from harm and those entrusted with the care of children are required to create and provide a safe environment for them. Every child has the right to be protected from physical or mental violence, injury or abuse, neglect or negligent treatment, maltreatment or exploitation, including sexual abuse.  
A safe child is not exposed to any form of discipline involving violence.   
A safe child has the freedom of expression and religion.

# 

# **Definitions** {#definitions}

Terms, perspectives, and views are complex and can differ across cultures. TYAS has adopted the definitions that follow:

## **Risk** {#risk}

Risk means the potential for something to go wrong (an incident or accident), such as the likelihood a student may be harmed or suffer adverse effects if exposed to a hazard.

## **Bullying** {#bullying}

Bullying is the repetitive, intentional hurting of one person or group by another person or group where the relationship involves an imbalance of power. It can be verbal, physical, or psychological. Bullying can happen face to face or online, which can lead or cause psychological damage. There are criminal laws in Taiwan which apply to harassment and threatening behavior. TYAS takes all incidents of bullying seriously. 

## **Physical abuse** {#physical-abuse}

A form of abuse that may involve hitting, shaking, throwing, poisoning, burning or scalding, drowning, suffocating, or otherwise causing physical harm to a child. Physical harm may also be caused by hitting or physically hurting a child as a form of punishment.

**Emotional abuse**  
The ongoing emotional harm to a child includes but is not limited to humiliating, belittling, bullying, shouting, and racial slanders. 

## **Neglect** {#neglect}

The failure to meet a child’s basic needs is likely to result in serious damage to the child’s health or development. Neglect includes the failure to properly supervise and protect children from harm.

## **Sexual abuse** {#sexual-abuse}

This involves forcing a child to take part in sexual activities, not necessarily involving a high level of violence, whether or not the child is aware of what is happening. This may include non-penetrative acts such as rubbing and touching outside of clothing. Activities may also include having children look at, or take part in the production of sexual images. Grooming with the intent of sexual abuse is also considered abuse, including online grooming. Adults and children of all ages can commit acts of sexual abuse.

# **Harassment and Abuse Policies** {#harassment-and-abuse-policies}

## **Relationships and Physical Contact** {#relationships-and-physical-contact}

TYAS condemns and expressly forbids any sexual contact or inappropriate relationships between adults and students and harassment of any kind. Employees engaging in any such inappropriate relationship will face severe disciplinary action, up to and including termination. It is never acceptable to put hands on a student in anger or with romantic intentions. This does not mean that we may never provide an appropriate pat on the back or accept an offered hug from a student. It does mean that touching a student in a way that results in the adult fulfilling his or her needs rather than encouraging a student is forbidden. All Employees are expected to promptly report to the Administration any inappropriate relationships between other Employees and students. All school staff are responsible for their own actions and behavior and should avoid any conduct that would lead any reasonable person to question their motivation and intentions.

## **Sexual Abuse and Harassment** {#sexual-abuse-and-harassment}

Prohibited sexual harassment includes sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature that has the purpose or effect of unreasonably interfering with a student's performance by creating an intimidating, hostile, humiliating, or sexually offensive working environment.  In addition, no staff member or student may sexually harass any student by making a submission to sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature either explicitly or implicitly. Any student who engages in any form of sexual harassment will face severe disciplinary action, up to and including expulsion. An Employee who engages in any form of sexual harassment shall be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.

## **Reporting and Responsibilities**

**Employees:**   
Must promptly report any incidents of bullying, hazing, harassment, or discrimination to the Administration. Failure to report such incidents may result in disciplinary actions, including verbal or written warnings, suspension without pay, or termination.

**Students**:   
Students are encouraged to report any incidents of bullying or harassment to a trusted adult, teacher, or school counselor. Anonymous reporting options are also available to ensure students feel safe coming forward. 

### 

### **Action Plan**

Step 1: Immediate Action  
\- Secure Safety-:Immediately ensure that all students involved are safe. Remove the victim from the situation and provide a safe space for them.  
\- Separate Students: Keep the alleged bully and the victim in separate spaces to prevent further conflict, ensuring that both are under adult supervision.

Step 2: Investigation  
\- Interviews: Conduct confidential interviews with the victim, the alleged bully, and any witnesses. Use open-ended questions to allow for detailed responses  
\- Document: Create a detailed report of the incident, noting the time, location, nature of the bullying, and identities of all involved parties and witnesses. Use objective language and stick to the facts.

Step 3: Inform-  
 \-Notify Staff: Immediately inform the principal, school counselor, and other relevant staff about the incident.  
\- Parent Communication: Call or meet with the parents of both the victim and the bully on the same day of the incident, if possible. Provide them with the facts of the case and explain the next steps.

Step 4: Support  
\- Assist the Victim: Offer immediate emotional support to the victim. Arrange for ongoing support, such as counseling sessions, peer support groups, or check-ins with a trusted staff member.  
\- Guidance for the Bully: Arrange for the bully to receive counseling, behavioral intervention, or other appropriate support to address and rectify their behavior.

Step 5: Discipline  
\- Enforce Consequences: Apply disciplinary measures in accordance with the school’s bullying policy. These could range from detention to suspension, based on the severity and frequency of the bullying.  
\- Restorative Practices: If suitable, organize restorative practices that involve structured meetings between the bully and the victim, facilitated by a trained mediator to foster understanding and healing.

Step 6: Education  
\- Reinforce Policies: Remind all students about the school’s anti-bullying policies through assemblies, classroom discussions, or digital communications.  
\- Preventive Education: Conduct educational sessions for students and staff to deepen their understanding of bullying impacts and prevention strategies.

Step 7: Follow-Up  
\- Monitor Progress: Continuously monitor the situation involving the bully and the victim to ensure no recurrence. This includes regular check-ins with both parties and their teachers.  
\- Adjust Supports: Modify the support measures provided to both the bully and the victim as necessary, based on feedback from them and their teachers or counselors.

Step 8: Review  
\- Evaluate Response: After the situation has been resolved, convene a meeting with the involved staff to evaluate the effectiveness of the response and to discuss any lessons learned.  
\- Policy Update: If necessary, update the school’s anti-bullying policy and response procedures to incorporate any new insights or strategies that emerged from handling the incident.

## This expanded action plan provides a clear and structured response to bullying, ensuring all parties are supported and that the school maintains a proactive stance against bullying behavior.

# **Reporting Safeguarding Issues** {#reporting-safeguarding-issues}

It is the professional duty of all staff to report welfare and safeguarding concerns to the designated senior person, or in their absence to the deputy designated person. Staff should only discuss concerns with the designated person**.** Remember, it is your responsibility to report your concerns. It is not your responsibility to investigate or decide whether a child has been abused.        

It takes a lot of courage for a child to disclose that they are being neglected and/or abused. They may feel ashamed, particularly if the abuse is sexual; their abuser may have threatened what will happen if they tell; they may have lost all trust in adults; or they may believe or have been told that the abuse is their own fault. Therefore, if a student raises an issue with you at any point, remember the following points:  

1. Allow them to speak freely, do not ask or lead questions.  
2. Remain calm and do not overreact – the child may stop talking if they feel they are upsetting you.   
3. Give reassuring nods or words of comfort – ‘I’m so sorry this has happened’, ‘I want to help’, ‘It isn’t your fault’, ‘You’re doing the right thing in talking to me’.   
4. Do not be afraid of silence – remember how hard this must be for the child.    
5. Reassure that all abusive behavior is not acceptable and it will not be tolerated.   
6. Do not automatically offer any physical touch as comfort. It may be anything but comforting to a child who has been abused.  
7. Avoid making promises that cannot be kept (‘Everything will be alright now’).   
8. Most importantly, teachers should never promise confidentiality.

# **The School’s Response Plan for Sexual Harassment Allegations**

**Stop Any Ongoing Harassment Immediately:** 

* React immediately to intervene and stop ongoing harassment.   
* Separate students are involved where applicable.  
* The student who has been targeted by harassment should not have to change his/her activities or move out of his/her classes to avoid further harassment.  
* Notify parents immediately.  
* Involve local law enforcement where applicable.


**Investigate All Allegations of Harassment:** 

* Take immediate action to investigate the incident regardless of whether the victim wants to file a formal complaint.  
* Collect evidence, witness reports, and other supporting items.

**Interviews:**

* Interview the harasser, and keep a complete record of the interview.  
* Interview the victim(s), and keep a complete record of the interview(s).  
* Interview witness(es), keep a complete record of the interview(s).  
* Decide on action steps to fit the severity of the conduct, and keep complete records of the action steps and implementation dates.  
* To ensure fairness, the person conducting the investigation should not be someone who works closely with or would be otherwise biased against any of the persons involved.  
  Action steps must aim to keep the victim safe now and in the future.

For the privacy of the students involved, limit the disclosure of information to people who really need to know, limit access to the investigation file and documents, and tell witnesses not to discuss the investigation with anyone.

**Take Corrective Action to Keep the Harassment from Happening Again:**   
If the alleged incidents of harassment are part of a larger problem that requires a school-wide response.  
Follow up with the student to make certain the harassment has stopped and that no retaliation has occurred;   
Refer and introduce the victim’s parents to a suitable counselor if needed.
